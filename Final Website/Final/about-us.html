About Us

This project examines how personalized recommendation systems can shape user behavior and gradually narrow the range of information a person encounters online. Many users begin with harmless interests such as gaming content, wellness routines, luxury lifestyle, or light-hearted conspiracy theory content. As platforms track these preferences, they begin to refine what appears on the screen. Over time, this creates a curated flow of material that feels aligned with the user’s tastes but quietly filters out alternative viewpoints. Initial interests can evolve into tightly contained echo chambers that limit critical thinking and increase vulnerability to more extreme ideas.

Research has documented these patterns across multiple online communities. Studies of the manosphere show clear links between repeated exposure and measurable shifts toward hostile and exclusionary language. Work on political personalization argues that recommendation systems contribute to ideological sorting by rewarding content that confirms a user’s existing assumptions. Additional research on digital radicalization highlights the risk that these systems can guide individuals toward more extreme material in gradual stages that are difficult to recognize while they are happening.

This project uses several entry points to illustrate these pathways and how all of them can be amplified through algorithmic selection and strengthened by repetitive engagement. The aim here is not to moralize individual choices, but to show how platform design can trap users in informational nets that feel self-directed despite being shaped by commercial and political incentives.

Because of these documented effects, there is a growing call for stronger regulation of digital environments. Possible measures include clearer transparency standards, oversight of recommender systems, and policy interventions that limit the reach of groups that encourage harmful or extremist behavior. Thoughtful legislation cannot eliminate online radicalization on its own, but it can help reduce the conditions that allow echo chambers to flourish and make users less likely to become isolated within them.

Sophia Andtbacka, cart 211, fall 2025, Final Website
